## Evidence note

An Agile logbook should record planning, progress, decisions, blockers, reviews, and learning over time. The supplied Trello CSV provides 85 task cards grouped into 13 sprint lists, with descriptions and user stories, but it does not provide a dependable dated activity history. This page is therefore a sprint-level summary and retrospective, not a fabricated diary with invented dates.

**Assessment assumption:** Non-legal work is treated as completed. The following remain outstanding: business registration, SARS tax registration, and business insurance.

## Retrospective

### What the project structure supports
- The board breaks the service concept into 13 named work areas.
- User stories connect tasks to needs such as client access, project submission, editing, invoicing, and launch testing.
- The backlog covers both customer-facing capabilities and supporting operations.
- Quality assurance, revisions, monitoring, and analytics are represented as work rather than being left implicit.

### What should improve
- The CSV is a task export, not proof that each card was completed on a particular date.
- Historical sprint dates, decisions, blockers, estimates, and review outcomes are not reliably evidenced in the export.
- Legal/compliance work needs explicit ownership, required evidence, and a verified completion check.
- High-impact features should have measurable acceptance criteria and dependencies.
- Any real project review should distinguish planned, in-progress, completed, and blocked work rather than relying on a general assumption.

### Actions for a future iteration
1. Record a dated sprint goal and the work selected at the start of each sprint.
2. Add owners, effort estimates, and acceptance criteria to tasks.
3. Capture blockers, decisions, and changes to scope as they happen.
4. Record sprint review feedback and retrospective actions.
5. Track useful measures such as turnaround time, revision frequency, client satisfaction, lead conversion, and payment status.
6. Verify business registration, SARS tax registration, and insurance before treating legal/compliance readiness as complete.

## Reflection

The logbook makes the project's progression and improvement opportunities visible without pretending that the Trello export contains a full history. It shows how planning connects to branding, the website, secure client access, project delivery, editing, payments, marketing, administration, and launch. The main lesson is that a board is most useful when it records not only what should be done, but also who owns it, when it was done, how it was checked, and what was learned. For this assessment, non-legal completion is an explicit assumption; the three legal/compliance tasks remain outstanding.
